Manmohan Singh

Dr. Manmohan Singh (1932–2024) was a distinguished Indian economist, bureaucrat, and statesman who served as the 13th Prime Minister of India (2004–2014). Widely recognized as the "architect of India's economic reforms," he introduced Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization (LPG) policies as Finance Minister in 1991, helping pull the nation out of an economic crisis. He was the first and only Sikh prime minister of India, representing the Indian National Congress.Early Life and Academic ExcellenceBorn in Gah, Punjab, in 1932, Dr. Singh rose from a humble background to excel academically, studying at Panjab University, Cambridge, and Oxford. He later taught economics at the Delhi School of Economics and served in top positions, including Governor of the Reserve Bank of India, before entering politics.Prime Minister of India (2004–2014)First Term (2004–2009): Appointed by the United Progressive Alliance (UPA) coalition, he was the first PM since Jawaharlal Nehru to be re-appointed after a full term. Key achievements included the Right to Information Act and the landmark Indo-US Civil Nuclear Deal.Second Term (2009–2014): Focused on infrastructure development but faced economic slowdowns and corruption challenges within the coalition.Legacy: His premiership was marked by significant economic growth and the introduction of major public welfare schemes, including MGNREGA.Later Life and PassingAfter retiring from active politics, he continued as a Rajya Sabha MP until 2024. Dr. Singh passed away on December 26, 2024, at the age of 92, leaving a legacy as a soft-spoken, intellectual leader who transformed the Indian economy.
